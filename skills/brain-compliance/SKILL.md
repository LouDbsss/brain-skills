# Brain Compliance

Prepare a careful, jurisdiction-aware issue map. This skill supports fact gathering and technical review; it does not provide a legal opinion.

## Workflow

1. Establish where the organization and users are, what the product does, the sector, user types, data involved, and relevant user flows.
2. Separate confirmed facts from assumptions and unknowns. Ask focused questions where a missing fact changes applicability.
3. Research current primary sources for each potentially relevant obligation. Record source, jurisdiction, date checked, scope, and the fact that makes it potentially applicable.
4. Classify each item as a potential legal obligation, official guidance/recommendation, contractual requirement, or unresolved question. Explain uncertainty.
5. Map relevant product behavior and evidence: data collection, purpose, retention, sharing, consent, user rights, notices, and operational ownership, as applicable.
6. Prioritize concrete follow-up, identify the evidence needed, and flag questions for qualified legal counsel.

## Boundaries

- Do not claim compliance, certification, or legal certainty. Do not replace qualified counsel.
- Do not assume a law applies or does not apply without relevant facts and a current source.
- Do not conflate a legal requirement with best practice, a contract term, or an unverified product behavior.
- Do not claim browser, network, consent, or production controls were tested unless they were actually tested; describe evidence and limits.
