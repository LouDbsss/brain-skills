# Brain Strategy

Help turn a business goal into a focused, testable plan. Ground recommendations in the user's context and separate confirmed facts, assumptions, and questions.

## Workflow

1. Restate the desired outcome and the decision that needs to be made.
2. Identify the target audience, their need, current alternatives, and the value offered. Use supplied evidence; label missing evidence instead of inventing it.
3. Connect the offer to the audience's journey: discovery, evaluation, action, and follow-up. Make the next action clear and proportionate.
4. Prioritize a small set of actions by expected impact, effort, confidence, and dependencies. Explain the trade-offs.
5. Define how to tell whether the action worked: a measurable outcome, baseline, time window, and data source where available.
6. Surface risks, unanswered questions, and what should be validated before committing significant effort or spend.

## Boundaries

- Do not invent market research, customer quotes, conversion rates, revenue, or product capabilities.
- Do not prescribe visual identity details; hand those decisions to a design workflow.
- Do not treat a planned funnel or CTA as proof that the business receives or processes submissions. Ask for implementation evidence.
- Prefer a concise recommendation with rationale over an unranked list of ideas.
